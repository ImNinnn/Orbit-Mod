Creator guide

Make a cosmetic

Orbit cosmetics are ordinary Blockbench models. If you can model in Blockbench, you can make one; no code, no resource packs.

Start from the template

  1. In game, press B to open the Orbit menu and click + at the top of the User Catalog.
  2. Orbit makes a new cosmetic, Orbit/Cosmetics/new_cosmetic/new_cosmetic.bbmodel, and opens its folder; open the file in Blockbench. It is already in your User Catalog.
  3. Model inside the body part groups (below). The locked REFERENCE_PLAYER body shows where the player is; it is never drawn in game.
  4. Save it with textures embedded. Rename the folder and the file to name it: the folder name is the cosmetic's name (top_hat/top_hat.bbmodel shows as "top hat").
  5. Open the Orbit menu again, select your cosmetic and click Equip.
Editing? Just save in Blockbench: Orbit notices new and changed files right away. You don't need to restart the game.

Body parts

Put geometry inside groups with these exact names (capital letters). Each one follows that part of the player, including when they nod, swing their arms or sneak.

GroupFollowsPivot in Blockbench
HEADHead: where the player looks0, 24, 0
BODYTorso0, 24, 0
RIGHT_ARMRight arm5, 22, 0
LEFT_ARMLeft arm-5, 22, 0
RIGHT_LEGRight leg1.9, 12, 0
LEFT_LEGLeft leg-1.9, 12, 0
anything elseThe player's position and body turn only0, 0, 0 (between the feet)

Keep each group's origin on its pivot (the template already does) and what you see on the reference player in Blockbench is what you get in game. One pixel in Blockbench is one pixel on the player.

Not drawn in game: the REFERENCE_PLAYER group, anything with Export turned off, faces without a texture, locators and null objects.

Animations

Make animations in Blockbench's Animate tab as usual: rotation, position and scale keyframes with linear, step, smooth or bezier curves, and Molang expressions. Then select the cosmetic in the Orbit menu and click Animations.

SettingWhat it does
PlaysIdle, Walk, Sprint, Jump, Fall, Sneak, Crouch, Swim, Fly, Always, Random or Never
ModeLoop; Once (then back to rest); Hold (stay on the last frame)
Min / max waitFor Random: the pause between plays, picked at random in this range (up to 120 s)
Speed0.1× to 4×
PriorityWhen two animations move the same part, the higher one wins
InterruptWhile playing, pauses this cosmetic's lower-priority animations

Name your animations IDLE, WALK, SPRINT, BACKWARDS, JUMP, FALL, SNEAK, CROUCH, SWIM, FLY, DAMAGE, ATTACK, MINE or RANDOM (or animation.hat.walk, run, hurt, dig, fidget and similar) and they are set up automatically. Walking backwards also counts as walking, and with nothing set to Sprint, the Walk animation keeps playing while sprinting. DAMAGE plays once when the player is hurt, ATTACK once when they hit a creature or player, and MINE while they break a block.

Animations that follow each other fade into one another (the Smooth animations setting, on by default), so your cosmetic never snaps from one pose to the next.

Movement physics

In the cosmetic's settings, the Velocity tab makes parts react to movement: a cape that lifts when running, a tail that swings when turning, ears that bounce when jumping. It works with or without animations.

Click Add part…, choose a group from the tree of your model's parts, and set any of position X/Y/Z and rotation X/Y/Z (Blockbench axes):

SettingWhat it does
Moves withForward (backward is negative), Sideways, Vertical, Any speed or Turning
StrengthPixels or degrees per block per second of movement; negative reverses it
Minimum, MaximumHow far it can move each way, in pixels or degrees. A minimum of 0 keeps it from going the negative way, like a chest lid that opens but never sinks into the chest
Resistance0 reacts instantly; close to 1 reacts late and settles smoothly

Up to 8 parts per cosmetic. A good start for a cape: rotation X, Forward, strength 8, minimum 0°, maximum 40°, resistance 0.6.

A player on the left wears the cosmetic: set its speed forward, sideways and up under it to see the parts react, while the background moves to show how fast it goes. Drag the player to turn it.

Body parts

Body parts replace parts of the player instead of adding to them: a pirate arm instead of yours, a robot head, or a whole new body. They work in the world, in menus, in first person (a replaced arm is your hand), during emotes and with model mods such as Entity Model Features and Fresh Animations.

In the menu's Body Parts section, click + for a template: the six parts of the player in the groups HEAD, BODY, RIGHT_ARM, LEFT_ARM, RIGHT_LEG and LEFT_LEG, mapped onto an empty PLAYER texture, so it looks exactly like you until you change it. Every group with something in it replaces that part, and moves like it.

  • Each part can be up to 50% bigger than the player's own part and cannot be empty; a part that breaks these rules is not used, and the player's own part shows.
  • Settings → Parts turns each part on or off; Particles adds particles. Body parts have no animations: they move with the player.

Any cosmetic can show the wearer's skin: a texture named PLAYER or SKIN, 64×64 and left empty, is drawn with the skin of whoever wears it. Map faces onto it like a skin.

Particles

Any group can emit particles. In game, open the cosmetic's Particles tab (for an emote, its Settings), click Add emitter and choose a group, up to 8. Particles appear where the group is, so animating the group moves where they appear. An empty group makes a good emitter.

The group's size sets how many particles it makes: at its normal size it emits normally, scaled down in an animation it emits less and less often, and at size 0 it stops. That is how you turn particles on and off during an animation.

Add a texture for them, such as particle.png (any texture in the file can be used; new emitters pick one with "particle" in its name). With alternate textures, particle_t1 becomes particle_t2 in the second style. Then click Edit. A player on the left wears it (or plays the emote) so you see the particles change as you edit them; the settings are split into Look and Motion:

SettingWhat it does
Texture, GlowWhich texture of the model it shows, and whether it is drawn at full brightness
Opacity, Lifetime, Fade outHow see-through it is, how long it lives, how long it fades at the end
Start size, End sizeA particle grows or shrinks from one to the other over its life, in pixels (16 to a block)
RotationEach particle starts turned by a random angle, up to this far either way
Spin min, maxHow fast it turns on itself: each particle picks a speed in between, in degrees per second
Wait min, maxA new particle comes after a random wait in between, in ticks (20 is a second)
Offset X/Y/ZParticles appear up to this far from the group either way, at random, in pixels
Speed X/Y/ZHow it moves, in blocks per second, relative to the player when it appears (Blockbench axes)
Turn X/Y/ZHow fast its direction turns, like steering a car: forward while turning around Y makes a circle
Speed change, Turn changeHow much those change every second; negative slows them down

Particles live in the world once they appear, so a moving player leaves a trail. Every Orbit player sees them, up to 20 per player at a time.

Alternate textures

A cosmetic can come in several styles, such as a backpack in different colours. Name its texture backpack_t1 and add the other styles as backpack_t2, backpack_t3 and so on, up to _t5, all the same size. Paint your faces with the _t1 texture; the others only need to exist in the file.

Several textures can change together: upperwings_t1, upperwings_t2, lowerwings_t1 and lowerwings_t2 make two styles. Textures without _t1 never change.

In the Orbit menu, hover the cosmetic's tile: a colour button for each style appears on its left, in the average colour of that style's texture. Other players see the style you chose.

Sharing your cosmetic

Your animation, physics and particle settings are saved in a .json file with the same name as the model, for example top_hat.bbmodel and top_hat.json. Share the cosmetic's folder and others get it exactly as you set it up; dropping the model onto the Orbit menu brings the settings along.

Add your name and a description to that file; a "name" there wins over the folder name:

{
  "orbitCosmetic": 1,
  "model": "top_hat.bbmodel",
  "name": "Top hat",
  "author": "Your name",
  "description": "A tall hat with a swinging brim.",
  "gradient": ["#3B82F6"],
  "animations": { ... },
  "physics": { ... },
  "particles": { ... }
}

"gradient" colours the cosmetic's tile in the Orbit menu: one colour the tile fades into, or two for the top and the bottom ("#RRGGBB", or "#AARRGGBB" with transparency). An emote has the same kind of .json, with its "mode" ("standard" or "mobile") and particles.

On a server, the cosmetics you wear are shared automatically with other Orbit players. You don't need to send files to anyone.

Emotes

An emote moves the player's own body. In the Orbit menu's Emotes section, click + for an emote template. Its groups hold the parts of the player (Export off, so they are only a guide):

ROOT             moves the whole player
├── LEFT_LEG
├── RIGHT_LEG
└── UPPERHALF    everything but the legs, bending at the hips
    ├── BODY
    ├── LEFT_ARM
    ├── RIGHT_ARM
    └── HEAD
  • Animate the groups in the Animate tab. Parts you don't animate keep their normal pose, so the head still follows where the player looks.
  • PLAY plays once; LOOP (or IDLE) loops until the player crouches (or moves, for Standard emotes). With both, PLAY plays first, then LOOP.
  • For emotes you can walk with: WALKING, SPRINTING and BACKWARDS loop instead of LOOP while the player moves that way (WALKING also while sprinting or going backwards when those are missing). An emote with them is always Mobile.
  • Anything else in the file with Export on, like a prop in a hand, is drawn while the emote plays.
  • The template is made straight in Orbit/Emotes/new_emote/; rename the folder and the file to name it (big_wave/big_wave.bbmodel is "big wave"), then put it on your wheel in the Orbit menu. Hold R in game to play it.

Limits

To keep everyone's game fast, models are checked before they are used. The default limits:

File size2 MiB (1 MiB to share through Orbit Cloud)
Cubes and meshes1,024
Faces6,144
Groups256, nested up to 16 deep
Textures16 (alternate textures included), each up to 512 × 512, 2 MB in all
Animations32

Orbit Cloud accepts files up to 1 MiB. The Orbit menu tells you which limit a model goes over (hover its red square).

Tips

  • Embed textures when you save (in Blockbench's texture menu) so your model is one self-contained file.
  • Fewer, bigger cubes are faster than many tiny ones. Hidden faces can have their texture removed.
  • Slightly larger than the body (inflate 0.25–0.5) avoids flickering where the cosmetic touches the skin.
  • Animated multi-frame textures, sounds, and Blockbench's own particle effects in animations aren't supported (make a group an emitter instead); the Orbit menu warns you.
  • Want a starting point? examples/orbit_demo.bbmodel in the repository has something on every body part.
